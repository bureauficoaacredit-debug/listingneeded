If it feels like new apartments are roomier than they used to be, RentCafe's latest size study backs you up, at least for much of the country.

## Average size is up

According to RentCafe's analysis of Yardi data, the average U.S. apartment is now **910 square feet**, up **13 square feet** from the year before. Every unit type gained space. RentCafe's averages:

- **Studios:** about 460 sq. ft.
- **One-bedrooms:** about 735 sq. ft.
- **Two-bedrooms:** about 1,095 sq. ft.
- **Three-bedrooms:** about 1,355 sq. ft., the biggest gain at 28 sq. ft. in a year

RentCafe connects the growth partly to people moving to Southern markets, where land costs less and floor plans tend to be larger.

## Where the biggest apartments are

RentCafe's study covered the 100 largest U.S. cities. **Tallahassee, FL** has the largest new apartments, averaging about 1,156 sq. ft. It's followed by **Gainesville, FL**, **Baton Rouge, LA**, **Knoxville, TN** and **Fort Myers, FL**.

## Where they're smallest, including our region

RentCafe says the smallest new apartments are mostly in Western and Northeastern cities. **Seattle** is the smallest at about 645 sq. ft. Closer to home, these cities appear on RentCafe's list of the 20 smallest:

- **Brooklyn, NY:** about 714 sq. ft.
- **Queens, NY:** about 732 sq. ft.
- **Manhattan, NY:** about 735 sq. ft.
- **Boston, MA:** about 747 sq. ft.
- **Jersey City, NJ:** about 785 sq. ft.

RentCafe explains that the trend toward building more studios and one-bedrooms in big cities keeps their averages down.

## What it means for Connecticut renters

Connecticut cities weren't broken out in this study. Still, the Northeast pattern is worth remembering: in dense, transit-friendly areas, you often trade space for location. Many Connecticut rentals are also in older multi-family houses, where layouts vary a lot from one home to the next.

## Tips for judging space when you tour

1. **Ask for the square footage** and how it was measured, then compare price per square foot between homes.
2. **Bring a tape measure** or use your phone to check where your bed, couch and desk will go.
3. **Count the closets and storage.** A smaller home with good storage can feel bigger than a larger one without it.
4. **Look at the layout, not just the number.** Long hallways count toward square footage but aren't living space.
