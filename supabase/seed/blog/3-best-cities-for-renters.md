If you're deciding where to rent next, RentCafe's yearly "best cities for renters" ranking is a handy way to think about the trade-offs. We read the 2026 edition and pulled out what's most useful for renters here in Connecticut.

## How RentCafe ranks cities

RentCafe looked at **20 metrics across 150 U.S. cities**, grouped into three areas:

- **Cost of living and housing.** Affordability, apartment size and similar measures.
- **Local economy.** Things like renter income growth and the job market.
- **Quality of life.** Walkability, transit, apartments in good locations and community life, including the number of social associations such as clubs and civic groups.

## The top of the list

According to RentCafe:

- **McKinney, TX** is the top city for renters again, followed by **Huntsville, AL** and **Austin, TX**.
- **San Marcos, TX** ranks first for cost of living and housing, **Miami** leads for local economy, and **Washington, D.C.** is first for quality of life.
- Southern cities dominate the top 50, with Florida, Texas and North Carolina the most represented states.

## The Northeast: strong on quality of life

RentCafe singles out the Northeast for **quality of life**. It points to good health care, universities, cultural amenities and some of the best public transit in the country. In the 2026 ranking, **Albany, NY** leads the region. **Boston** and **Manhattan** stand out in the quality-of-life category, ranking 14th and 3rd. RentCafe notes that Manhattan scores well there even though it doesn't make the overall top 50.

## Using the same yardsticks in Connecticut

You don't need a national ranking to use RentCafe's approach. When you compare towns or neighborhoods, ask yourself the same three questions:

1. **Cost.** What's the total monthly cost, including utilities, parking and renter's insurance, not just the rent?
2. **Economy.** How long is the commute to your job? Is there a train station? On the shoreline and in Fairfield County, access to Metro-North can matter as much as the apartment itself.
3. **Quality of life.** Can you walk to groceries, parks or a downtown? Is there a community you'd like to be part of?

A slightly higher rent can still be the better deal if it saves you a car payment or an hour of commuting each day.
